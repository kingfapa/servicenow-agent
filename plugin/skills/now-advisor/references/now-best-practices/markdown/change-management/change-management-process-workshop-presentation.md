## Slide 19

What Change Management is not

Release Management – The change process is not intended to manage the planning, building, testing, and deployment of software. It is purely to provide permission to deploy the software into a live environment and ensure the visibility of that work

Demand Management – The change process is not intended to request enhancements to products. It should only be used to provide permission to put the enhancement live into the product once it gets to that stage

Request Management – The change process is not intended to make requests for updates that do not require the need for risk assessment, CI impact, and scheduling

Change Management overview

### Speaker notes

Explain and discuss

Explain that many customers have created very complicated custom Change processes in the past because they have not understood what Release Management is. Those customers are now trying to remove all the unnecessary complexity and follow a true Release Management process. Trying to incorporate Release Management activities into Change tends to result in the process not working for infrastructure changes and generally becoming extremely messy and hard to follow. There is a dedicated Release Management product for this purpose

Explain that the same applies to Demand Management. The Change Management process is not intended to be used for these types of activities. There is a dedicated Demand Management product for this purpose.

## Slide 28

Traditionally, as characterized by ITIL, there has been a change type which generally falls into 3 types

Emergency (Typically caused by an incident)

Standard (Preapproved changes that all follow the same model)

Normal (Covering the majority of other changes)

A limited set of change types is becoming less relevant as change enablement scales and the velocity of change increases. Enterprise Change teams serve a wide range of enterprise constituents, and there is an increasing need for out-of-the-box Changes that can be adapted to various common use cases, supporting business agility and enabling digital transformation. These are Change Models.

Change Models allow for custom state transition, flow design, and subsequent custom approval policy.

A model shall be one of the 3 change types

Note: Change templates can be configured for all change models, with field policies for mandatory and read-only fields, template categories, and a managed lifecycle (propose, review, version, retire).

Change Model and change types overview

### Speaker notes

Explain and discuss

A model-based approach to change enables the change process to adapt to new, modern ways of working and simplifies the existing ITIL change implementations; ultimately this increases change velocity and volume while simultaneously improving stability and governance.

Discuss Digital transformation initiatives and IT strategic approach for change

Mode 1 change is based on a linear, predictable approach to change which produces reliable IT deliverables and is valued via cost.

Mode 2 change is based on an exploratory and experimental approach (agile) to change driven by agility and speed of SDLC and valued by customer experience, reputation and revenue – Mode 2 is the recommended greenfield approach from ServiceNow.

NOTE: Traditional change types (and matching models) can definitely exist alongside of tailored models (for DevOps/CICD/automated requirements)

Decisions to make

Confirm the customer requires/desires multimodal change or is satisfied with traditional change type model alone. Greenfield customers should be strongly discouraged from opting for Mode 1 Change as they will begin to run into challenges as they begin to automate and scale change volumes, as is the common trend amongst organizations.

Recommend

Customers thoroughly understand the architecture of development and infrastructure processes and engage with stakeholders before taking on multimodal change models.

Functional Details –

As a Change manager, I can select from an inventory of Change models that can be easily tailor to my organization’s appetite for risk.

### Speaker notes

Explain and discuss

Review each of the Change types

It is recommended that change models be used where additional types are required for normal changes

Normal: Normal Changes follow the complete Change lifecycle and proceed through all steps of the process, including peer or technical approval, Change Management approval, and Change Advisory Board (CAB) authorization before being implemented, reviewed, and closed.

Standard: Standard Changes are often considered routine. This is a Change that is frequently implemented, has repeatable implementation steps, is considered low risk, and has a proven history of success. Standard Changes are pre-approved and follow a shorter lifecycle.

Emergency: Emergency Changes cover both fixes ON fail or retrospective situations where the impact to service has already been experienced, and fix OR fail situations where the impact to service is imminent if action is not taken. These Changes do not follow the complete lifecycle of a normal Change due to the speed with which they must be authorized. Therefore, they progress directly to Change Management and ECAB authorization.
