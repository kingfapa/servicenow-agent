### Speaker notes

Explain and discuss

Explain watermelon SLA effect, green on the outside, red in the middle.

Traditional SLA’s have been based on individual activities such as incident resolution times or system availability. These do not have a business context or outcome associated with them.  For example, if the availability of a service is 99.9%, but if the 0.1% fails during an important processing happing such as a marketing push, or black Friday then the customer experience will be low, regardless of the SLA has been met.

Explain that to gain full SLAs against the service is to gather not just operational metrics, but customer and business metrics too.

SLAs against customer experience of the service

SLAs are not typically negotiated or fully consulted with representatives from the business (the people that use the IT service)

This is what ITIL describes as the “co-creation of value through service relationship” in which “customers are an essential element in the process of creating value”.

Today, a more meaningful use of SLAs is about measuring business processes mapped to business outcomes.

Explain how effective IT organizations must change how they measure in order to be innovative and show the organizations they support how much they’re contributing to business outcomes.

Explain how the ‘outcome SLA’ is combination of multiple sources

This hierarchy combines the traditional ‘ITIL®’ SLM concepts with the modern SRE/DevOps concepts.

SLM should be used alongside a wider customer success program considering transactional and periodic feedback, business objectives etc. and should be considered a component of XLAs (eXperience Level Agreements)

SLAs are the overall agreement you have with a customer, user or client that can be made up of one or more SLOs, e.g. server uptime, website availability

SLOs are the objectives your team need to meet to align with that SLA; these are represented by OLAs with internal teams or underpinning contracts with external suppliers. Try and focus on measuring the SLOs that really matter rather than trying to measure them all

SLIs are the metrics that measure compliance with your SLOs, for example if you have an SLA/SLO that requires 99.9% uptime, the SLI is the actual measurement of that.

### Speaker notes

Explain

The maturity model for managing SLAs looks like this:

Crawl – using the default, out of the box SLA for incident response. You may also choose to use this for request fulfilment. This sets a single expectation for customers on the service they receive and is the bare minimum. This often does not take into account third party SLAs (OLAs to you) and as a result these may be longer than the SLA you promise to your customer. You will likely be pausing the SLAs when a task is with a third party. You are monitoring SLA performance at a high level using basic out of the box dashboard and reporting.

Walk – here you are defining specific SLAs with your customers for the most important/common requests and service offerings. You are providing customers and measuring against a target response time, i.e. when their enquiry will first be looked at by a resolver or fulfilment group. You are providing customers with an estimated fulfilment time when ordering an item through the service catalog. You have recorded key supplier contracts and SLAs within the platform.

Run – You have defined SLAs specific to all service offerings and requests, taking into account the SLAs you have with dependent third parties and suppliers. You no longer need to place tasks on hold when awaiting a supplier, as the time for suppliers to complete tasks is built in to your overall SLA. You are beginning to use tools such as Digital Portfolio Management to measure SLA performance by portfolio, service and offering.

Fly – You are committing to advanced SLA metrics such as RTO and RPO. You have all of your supplier service contracts defined which support your overall SLAs.

Discuss

Where are you on this journey, what is your starting point? What is the target state post implementation?

Actions

Customer to provide relevant information based on target maturity state post implementation, for example, agree SLA matrixes, agree behavior of SLA pause on ‘on hold’ states, information to populate service offering /service catalog records

## Slide 18

Site Reliability Engineering (SRE)

A SRE team is responsible for change management, emergency response, monitoring, availability, performance, latency, efficiency, and capacity planning of the services, usually writing software for process automation.

Site reliability engineers can register services, monitor service health, and respond to alerts and incidents effectively with the Site Reliability Operations application. SRE is linked to DevOps

From an SLM perspective, the Error Budget is a key metric

Error budgets are the tool SRE uses to balance service reliability with the pace of innovation. Changes are a significant source of instability. The error budget forms a control mechanism for diverting attention to stability as needed.

Example:  An error budget is 1 minus the SLO of the service. A 99.9% SLO service has a 0.1% error budget. If a service receives 10000 requests in four weeks, a 99.9% availability SLO gives us a budget of 10 errors over that period.

## Slide 20

SLA Considerations

SLAs should be determined based on a combination of factors, including business objectives, customer needs, impact on business operations, resource availability, and interdependencies between services.

Align with business objectives

The SLAs should be aligned to the business goals they are designed to support. Set realistic and measurable targets. To achieve this business stakeholders should be involved in the development of SLAs

Impact on business operations

SLA priorities and handling should consider the impact of that service outage on the business operations

Include clear escalation procedures.

When an SLA is not met (breached) what action should be taken

Determine interdependencies

The priorities of each SLA should consider any interdependencies between services.

Contractual Obligations

SLAs should ensure that contractual obligations can be measured

Avoid creating SLAs for coverage – focus on critical customer journey touchpoints: Less is more

### Speaker notes

Explain and Discuss

=================

Many organizations stop the clock when certain triggers are met (for example a work item being assigned to a third party or when awaiting a customer response). This creates a discrepancy between the true end-to-end duration of a task and the SLA timer, known as the ‘watermelon SLA’ effect. Although reporting shows the task as well within the SLA, the true customer experience is not reflected the same. Consider carefully what meets your criteria for pausing the SLA. Although it may not be possible immediately, as you mature you should look to ensure third party SLAs are within your total SLA per service offering and by request. This sets a much more accurate and transparent expectation with a customer.

For example:

A new laptop order SLA is 6-10 days. This includes the supplier SLA of 5-9 days plus a day to include the receipt and processing of the request internally.

Incidents affecting availability of the ‘Email’ service offering quote a 8 hour response time, including the 4 hour supplier fix SLA and time each side for internal logging, assignment and customer contact.

Discuss

As of today, what are the acceptable scenarios in which you are happy to stop the SLA clock? Does this apply to incidents and requests? Do you wish to include problems as well?

What does an ideal future state look like – could this change?

SLA Components

There are four major components that work together to power the Service Level Agreements Plugin

Schedule and Schedule Entry records define the hours that SLA’s are in effect.

For 24x7 operations, no schedule is used.

Schedule Entries are exceptions to normal SLA hours, for example, Holiday &amp; Weekend Hour schedules.

Child Schedule entries are attached to Schedules to identify when SLA’s do not accumulate time.

SLA Definition: the record which defines the conditions that trigger the SLA.

Includes link to schedules to control when time is counted toward SLA &amp; When time is stopped.

SLA Workflow: the workflow which powers events or actions based on the SLA definition.

## Slide 27

SLA Definition – Start Condition

Enables you to define the conditions under which the SLA will be attached.

You can choose the conditions from the When to cancel list under which the SLA will be cancelled.

Start conditions are not met option: If one or more of the specified start conditions change, then the SLA will be cancelled. The Start conditions are not met option is selected by default.

Cancel conditions are met option: The start condition has to be met only once, thereafter the SLA will only cancel when the cancel condition is met.

Never option: The SLA will never be cancelled.

Select Retroactive start to choose start from “Set start to” field

## Slide 28

SLA Definition – Pause Condition

Pause conditions are not met option: If one or more of the specified pause conditions no longer match, then the elapsed time will continue to increase. The Pause conditions are not met option is selected by default.

Resume conditions are met option: If one or more of the specified resume conditions match, then the elapsed time will continue to increase

## Slide 29

SLA Definition – Stop Condition

Enables you to define the conditions under which the SLA completes. If all of the specified stop conditions match, then the task SLA will complete regardless of whether it is breached.

## Slide 30

SLA Definition – Reset Condition

It enables you to define the conditions under which the running SLA will be completed and a new SLA attached. The start condition must match for a new SLA to be attached.

The Reset condition also helps configure SLAs when the value of any specific field on the task record changes, changes to, or changes from a specific value in the record.

What are services and service offerings?

A service is a means of delivering value to customers by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve without owning the costs and risks.

A service offering consists of one or more service commitments that uniquely define the level of service in terms of availability, scope, pricing, and packaging options. Customers can choose to receive different levels of performance and features for a given service through service offerings (typically made available as distinct items in the service catalog).

A service can have multiple service offerings (each made available as separate catalog items) with different service level agreements (SLAs).

How is a service different from a project?

Project

Service

This is a point-in-time effort to build new functionality or deliver a business change, such as deploying a new application.

This is the ongoing management and enhancement of existing functionality or a business activity, like server management.

Success is measured in terms of project delivery (schedule, budget, scope).

Success is measured in terms of ongoing service performance (quality, cost, demand, and business impact).

A project is managed by a project manager who moves to a new project once the current project is complete.

A service is managed by a service manager who owns the entire service lifecycle from launch to retirement.

## Slide 68

FAQ

What is the difference between "Actual elapsed time" and "Business elapsed time"?

The "Actual" fields contain times that are always calculated on a 24 \* 7 basis. These fields indicate the physical time that has elapsed since the Task SLA record was created.

The "Business" fields contain the timings based on the Schedule that is associated with the Task SLA record. Schedules define a set of working or business hours.

Why are the "Actual elapsed time" and "Business elapsed time" not correct on the Task SLA record?

The timings and percentages on the Task SLA records are not calculated and updated on a continuous basis as it can create performance overhead in system. The SLA Engine takes a "just-in-time" approach to calculate and update these values, as needed.

What is the purpose of the "SLA Due", "Made SLA" and "Escalation" fields on the Task record?

The fields "SLA Due", "Made SLA" and "Escalation" are legacy fields that are not used by the 2010 or 2011 versions of the SLA Engine.
